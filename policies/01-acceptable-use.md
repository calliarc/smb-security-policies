## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how people at {{COMPANY_NAME}} may use company devices, accounts, networks and data. The aim is simple: let everyone work productively while keeping customer data, company data and our systems out of trouble. It sets out what is expected, what is not allowed, and what happens when something goes wrong.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All employees, contractors, interns, temporary staff and volunteers ("users").
- All company-owned or company-managed devices: laptops, desktops, phones, tablets, and peripherals.
- Personal devices when they are used to access company accounts or data (see also the [Remote Work and BYOD Policy](08-remote-work-byod.md)).
- All company accounts and services, including email, chat, file storage, line-of-business applications and cloud platforms.
- Company networks, both office and guest Wi-Fi.

## 4. Policy statements

### 4.1 General principles

1. Users must use company systems and data primarily for legitimate business purposes.
2. Limited personal use (for example, checking personal email over lunch) is allowed if it does not interfere with work, consume significant resources, break the law, or break any other part of this policy.
3. Users must not expect privacy when using company systems. {{COMPANY_NAME}} may monitor and log activity on company systems for security, legal and operational reasons, within the limits of applicable law.
4. Users must read and acknowledge this policy before receiving access, and again after each material update.

### 4.2 Accounts and credentials

1. Users must only use accounts that have been issued to them.
2. Users must not share passwords, MFA codes, security keys or session links with anyone, including colleagues, managers or IT staff.
3. Users must follow the [Password and MFA Policy](02-password-and-mfa.md).
4. Users must not try to bypass, disable or weaken authentication or access controls.

### 4.3 Devices

1. Users must keep company devices physically secure and must not leave them unattended in public places or visible in unattended vehicles.
2. Users must lock their screen when stepping away. Devices must be configured to lock automatically after no more than 15 minutes of inactivity (5 minutes or less for phones and tablets is recommended).
3. Users must not disable or tamper with security software, including endpoint protection, disk encryption, device management agents, or automatic updates.
4. Users must install operating system and application updates when prompted, and should restart devices at least weekly so that updates can finish.
5. Users must not let family members or other unauthorised people use company devices.
6. Users must report lost or stolen devices to {{IT_HELPDESK}} as soon as possible and within 24 hours at the latest.

### 4.4 Software and cloud services

1. Users must only install software from approved sources (the company app catalogue, an official vendor site, or an approved app store).
2. Users must not sign up for new cloud services, browser extensions or AI tools that will store or process company data without approval from {{IT_PROVIDER}} or {{SECURITY_LEAD}}. This includes free tiers.
3. Users must not use unlicensed, pirated or cracked software.
4. Users should check the approved software list at {{INTRANET_URL}} before requesting something new.

### 4.5 Generative AI tools

1. Users must only use AI assistants and similar tools that {{COMPANY_NAME}} has approved for business use.
2. Users must not enter Confidential or Restricted information (see the [Data Classification and Handling Policy](07-data-classification-and-handling.md)) into any AI tool unless that tool has been approved for that classification.
3. Users must review AI-generated output for accuracy before relying on it or sending it to customers, and remain responsible for work they submit.

### 4.6 Email, messaging and the web

1. Users must treat unexpected links, attachments, QR codes, and requests for payment, gift cards or credentials with suspicion, even when they appear to come from a colleague or known supplier.
2. Users must verify any request to change bank details, make an urgent payment, or share sensitive data using a known phone number or another separate channel, not by replying to the request itself.
3. Users must report suspected phishing using {{PHISHING_REPORT_METHOD}}.
4. Users must not auto-forward company email to personal accounts.
5. Users must not use personal email, personal messaging apps or personal file-sharing accounts to conduct company business or store company data.
6. Users must not access, store or share content that is illegal, harassing, discriminatory, sexually explicit or otherwise inappropriate for the workplace.

### 4.7 Data handling

1. Users must handle data according to its classification, as set out in the [Data Classification and Handling Policy](07-data-classification-and-handling.md).
2. Users must store company files in approved company locations (for example, {{FILE_STORAGE}}), not on local drives only or on removable media.
3. Users must not copy company data to USB drives or other removable media unless there is a business need and the media is encrypted and approved.
4. Users must only access data they need for their job, even if technical permissions would allow more.

### 4.8 Networks

1. Users must not connect unapproved networking equipment (such as wireless access points, routers or network sniffers) to the company network.
2. Users should avoid untrusted public Wi-Fi for company work. When it cannot be avoided, users must use {{VPN_OR_SECURE_ACCESS}} where required, or a personal mobile hotspot.
3. Guest Wi-Fi is for visitors and personal devices only. Company devices should use the corporate network.

### 4.9 Prohibited activities

Users must not:

1. Scan, probe or test the security of any system, internal or external, without written authorisation from {{SECURITY_LEAD}}.
2. Attempt to access accounts, data or systems they have not been authorised to use.
3. Introduce malware or knowingly run untrusted code on company systems.
4. Use company systems for personal commercial activity, cryptocurrency mining, or political campaigning.
5. Infringe copyright or other intellectual property, including by sharing licensed content or software without permission.
6. Make statements on behalf of {{COMPANY_NAME}} in public forums or on social media without authorisation.

### 4.10 Reporting

1. Users must report suspected security incidents, policy violations, lost devices, or accidental data disclosures immediately to {{SECURITY_EMAIL}} or {{IT_HELPDESK}}.
2. Reporting a mistake quickly is always better than hiding it. {{COMPANY_NAME}} will not discipline anyone for promptly reporting an honest mistake.

### 4.11 Leaving the company

1. Users must return all company devices, security keys, badges and other assets on or before their last day.
2. Users must not keep, copy or delete company data when they leave, except as instructed by their manager.
3. Confidentiality obligations continue after employment ends, in line with each user's contract.

## 5. Exceptions

Requests for exceptions must be submitted in writing to {{SECURITY_LEAD}} and include the business reason, the systems affected, compensating controls, and a proposed end date. Approved exceptions are recorded in the exception register and reviewed at least every 12 months or at their end date, whichever is sooner.

## 6. Enforcement

Violations may lead to removal of access and disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment or contract, in line with {{COMPANY_NAME}}'s disciplinary procedure and local law. Illegal activity may be reported to law enforcement. The severity of the response will reflect intent, impact and whether the issue was reported promptly.

## 7. Review cadence

{{POLICY_OWNER}} reviews this policy at least annually, and after any significant incident, major change in technology (for example, adopting a new collaboration platform), or change in legal requirements.
